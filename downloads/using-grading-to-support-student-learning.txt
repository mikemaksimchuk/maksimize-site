1️⃣A Grading and Assessment Model that Supports
Chapter 1: A Grading and Assessment Model that Supports Student
Learning
Big Idea
Grading systems should accurately communicate student learning and support improvement rather than simply
accumulate points.
Reflection Questions
● How does your current grading system communicate student learning to students and families?
● In what ways might point accumulation obscure actual student understanding?
● Reflect on how grades influence student motivation and engagement in your classroom.
Application Activities
● Audit your current gradebook categories and identify which ones represent learning vs compliance.
● Develop a simple model that separates formative practice from summative evaluation.
2️⃣Grading and Homework: Moving from Points
Chapter 2: Grading and Homework: Moving from Points to Practice
Big Idea
Homework should be treated as practice that supports learning rather than as a graded measure of achievement.
Reflection Questions
● What role does homework currently play in your grading system?
● How might grading homework influence student risk-taking or willingness to practice challenging
skills?
● What evidence do you have that homework grades accurately reflect learning?
Application Activities
● Pilot a “practice not points” homework model for one unit.
● Collect student feedback about how homework expectations influence their effort and learning.
3️⃣Grading and Formative Assessment: Transitioning
Chapter 3: Grading and Formative Assessment: Transitioning from
Points to Preview
Big Idea
Formative assessments should provide insight into learning progress without heavily impacting grades.
Reflection Questions
● Reflect on how formative assessments currently affect student grades in your classroom.
● How might separating formative feedback from grading change student engagement?
● What evidence do formative assessments provide about student learning needs?
Application Activities
● Redesign a formative assessment so it provides feedback without contributing to the final grade.
● Use formative data to adjust instruction in the next lesson.
4️⃣Grading and Summative Assessment:
Chapter 4: Grading and Summative Assessment: Migrating from Points
to Proficiency
Big Idea
Summative assessments should measure proficiency relative to learning standards rather than simply assigning
points.
Reflection Questions
● How do your current summative assessments communicate levels of mastery?
● What challenges arise when translating complex learning into a single score or grade?
● How might proficiency scales improve clarity for students and families?
Application Activities
● Create a proficiency scale for a unit standard.
● Analyze student work to determine what proficiency levels look like in practice.
5️⃣Standards-Based Grading
Chapter 5: Standards-Based Grading
Big Idea
Standards-based grading focuses on communicating what students know and can do relative to clearly defined
learning standards.
Reflection Questions
● How might standards-based grading improve clarity about student learning?
● What barriers exist in your context to implementing standards-based grading practices?
● How might grading reforms influence student motivation and classroom culture?
Application Activities
● Develop a standards-aligned gradebook category for a unit.
● Communicate the proficiency scale to students and families and gather feedback.

Final Reflection and Action Plan
Synthesis
Effective grading practices prioritize clarity, accuracy, and communication about student learning. When grades
are aligned with learning goals and supported by meaningful formative feedback, they become a tool for
guiding improvement rather than merely ranking performance.
Reflection
● What are my three key takeaways from this book study?
● What grading practice would most benefit from revision in my classroom?
