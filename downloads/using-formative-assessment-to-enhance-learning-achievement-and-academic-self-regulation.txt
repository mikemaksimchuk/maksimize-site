Thank you for participating in this professional learning experience.
This study plan follows the five chapters of Using Formative Assessment to
Enhance Learning, Achievement, and Academic Self-Regulation by Heidi Andrade
and Margaret Heritage. The book explores how formative assessment practices can
strengthen student learning while helping students develop the ability to monitor,
regulate, and take ownership of their learning.
Participants are encouraged to engage with the book as part of this experience. You
may borrow a copy, access it in a way that works for you, or participate fully
through the reflection prompts and application activities provided here. Engagement
with the ideas (and reflection on your own teaching context) is what matters most.
This professional learning experience centers on the idea that formative assessment
supports learning when teachers and students work together to clarify goals, gather
evidence of understanding, interpret feedback, and take action. When students
become active participants in this process, they develop self-regulation skills that help them become more
independent learners.

1️⃣Introduction to Formative Assessment
Chapter 1: Introduction to Formative Assessment
Big Idea
Formative assessment is a continuous process in which teachers and students gather and use evidence of
learning to guide instructional decisions and improve learning outcomes.
Reflection Questions
● How is formative assessment currently used in your classroom?
● In what ways do students participate in assessment conversations about their learning?
● What evidence do you typically collect to determine whether students are progressing toward learning
goals?
● How might formative assessment practices influence student motivation and engagement?
Application Activities
● Identify three points during a lesson where formative assessment could occur.
● Implement a quick formative assessment strategy (exit ticket, questioning sequence, think-pair-share).
● Reflect on how the evidence collected influenced instructional decisions.
2️⃣Actionable Principles of Formative Assessment
Chapter 2: Actionable Principles of Formative Assessment
Big Idea
Effective formative assessment follows a set of principles that guide teachers in designing learning experiences
that provide meaningful feedback and support student learning.
Reflection Questions
● What principles currently guide assessment practices in your classroom?
● How do formative assessment practices support students’ confidence and motivation?
● In what ways do your classroom routines encourage or discourage formative assessment conversations?
● How might formative assessment practices influence students’ beliefs about their ability to learn?
Application Activities
● Identify a formative assessment practice you already use and analyze how it aligns with key formative
assessment principles.
● Design a classroom routine that supports ongoing assessment conversations.
● Share formative assessment strategies with colleagues and gather feedback.
3️⃣Clear Learning Goals and Criteria
Chapter 3: Clear Learning Goals and Criteria
Big Idea
Students learn more effectively when they clearly understand learning goals and the criteria used to evaluate
their progress.
Reflection Questions
● Reflect on how learning goals are communicated in your classroom.
● How clearly do students understand what success looks like for a given task?
● What role do rubrics, checklists, or success criteria play in helping students understand expectations?
● How might involving students in creating criteria influence their engagement?
Application Activities
● Rewrite a learning goal in student-friendly language.
● Develop success criteria for an upcoming assignment.
● Invite students to co-construct criteria for evaluating a project or performance task.
4️⃣Collecting and Interpreting Evidence of Learning
Chapter 4: Collecting and Interpreting Evidence of Learning
Big Idea
Teachers and students must gather meaningful evidence of learning and interpret it accurately to inform next
instructional steps.
Reflection Questions
● What kinds of evidence do you collect to understand student learning during instruction?
● How do you determine whether the evidence collected accurately reflects student understanding?
● What role do self-assessment and peer assessment play in interpreting evidence of learning?
● How might technology tools support collecting evidence of learning?
Application Activities
● Design a formative assessment activity that allows students to demonstrate their understanding in
multiple ways.
● Implement a student self-assessment routine using a rubric or checklist.
● Use assessment evidence to adjust an upcoming lesson.
5️⃣Taking Action
Chapter 5: Taking Action
Big Idea
Formative assessment is most effective when teachers and students take action based on the evidence gathered.
Reflection Questions
● Reflect on how instructional decisions are influenced by assessment evidence in your classroom.
● How often do students revise work or adjust strategies after receiving feedback?
● What opportunities exist for students to reflect on their learning and identify next steps?
● How might feedback support both learning improvement and self-regulation?
Application Activities
● Provide targeted feedback on a student assignment focused on next steps for improvement.
● Create a reflection prompt asking students to identify how they will apply feedback.
● Design a revision opportunity that allows students to improve their work.
🏁 Final Reflection and Action Plan
Final Reflection and Action Plan
Synthesis
Formative assessment is most powerful when teachers and students share responsibility for monitoring learning
progress. By clearly defining learning goals, gathering meaningful evidence, interpreting feedback, and taking
action, educators create learning environments where assessment becomes a tool for growth rather than
judgment. When students actively participate in formative assessment processes, they develop self-regulation
skills that help them plan, monitor, and evaluate their own learning.
