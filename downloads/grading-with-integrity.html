"Grading with Integrity" By Thomas Guskey PhD, Nancy Frey PhD, and Doug Fisher PhD Thank you for joining us on this journey! This study plan is designed for educators to delve into the principles and practices discussed in "Grading with Integrity." The plan spans the eight chapters, with each chapter dedicated to a specific area of focus. Participants will engage in reading, self-reflection, and application activities to deepen their understanding and prepare for implementation of the concepts presented. The following session outline encourages practical application of the concepts in the text, as well as self-reflection on current practices within the district. Remember to take notes as you read, and reflect on how the information can be applied to your individual district or setting. Participants are encouraged to engage in discussions with peers or colleagues to further enrich their understanding, or engage colleagues in the suggested activities. Chapter 1 – Why Grading for Integrity?

Reflection Prompts

  • What are your initial reactions to the concept of grading with integrity?
  • Reflect on any personal or professional experiences where grading practices felt unfair or misleading.
  • How might grading with integrity enhance student motivation and learning? Activities:
  • Write a brief reflection identifying your personal "why" behind wanting to improve grading practices.
  • Conduct a preliminary audit of your current grading practices and note potential areas for improvement.

Chapter 2 – A Brief History of Grading: How Did We Get Here?

Reflection Prompts

  • How have historical grading practices influenced your current grading methods?
  • What surprised you most about the evolution of grading practices described in the chapter?
  • How do historical grading practices continue to impact students today? Activities:
  • Create a timeline of significant historical developments in grading practices to share with your colleagues.
  • Reflect and discuss with colleagues how historical grading practices might still influence your school's current approach.

Chapter 3 – Asking Why: What Is Our Purpose for Grading?

Reflection Prompts

  • What do you see as the primary purpose of grading?
  • How does your grading practice align or conflict with your stated purpose?
  • What shifts might you need to make to align grading with clearly articulated purposes? Activities:
  • Draft or revise a grading purpose statement for your classroom or school.
  • Hold a virtual or in-person discussion with colleagues about different purposes of grading and record key insights.

Chapter 4 – Current Grading Schemas: What Are the Existing Problems?

Reflection Prompts

  • Which common grading practices described in this chapter resonate with your experience as problematic?
  • How do existing grading schemas potentially misrepresent student learning?
  • What existing grading practices in your school might inadvertently disadvantage certain student populations? Activities:
  • Evaluate and critique a specific grading schema currently used at your school, identifying its strengths and weaknesses.
  • Develop recommendations for addressing identified grading issues and share these with colleagues or school leaders.

Chapter 5 – Product: What is Standards-Based or Competency-Based

Grading?

Reflection Prompts

  • How familiar are you with standards-based or competency-based grading?
  • What potential benefits and challenges do you foresee in implementing standards-based grading?
  • Reflect on how transitioning to a standards-based system might impact your students' learning and motivation. Activities:
  • Research and summarize examples of standards-based grading policies from other schools or districts.
  • Create or revise an assessment rubric aligned to specific standards or competencies you currently teach.

Chapter 6 – Process: How Do We Include Nonacademic Learning Goals in

Grading?

Reflection Prompts

  • How do your current grading practices handle nonacademic factors like effort, attendance, or participation?
  • Why is it important to report nonacademic goals separately from academic achievement?
  • How might clearly separating academic from nonacademic goals improve equity in your grading practices? Activities:
  • Develop a separate reporting tool or rubric for nonacademic learning goals.
  • Identify specific nonacademic learning goals relevant to your teaching context and describe how you could monitor and report on them independently from academic grades.

Chapter 7: Progress: How Do We Report Growth and Improvement?

Reflection Prompts

  • How do you currently document or acknowledge student growth in your classroom or school?
  • What challenges have you encountered when trying to measure or communicate progress over time?
  • Ipsative assessments compare a student’s current performance with their past work. How might this approach shift your grading or feedback practices?
  • Which of the reporting tools described in this chapter (for example, descriptive feedback, low-stakes grading, portfolios, formative practice) do you already use, and which are new or underutilized?
  • How might a stronger focus on documenting student growth affect student motivation and classroom culture? Activities:
  • Design a Low-Stakes Grading System
  • Develop a plan for implementing low-stakes practice assignments in a current unit.
  • Include how you will give feedback, how these tasks will (or won’t) influence grades, and how you’ll monitor progress over time.
  • Pilot a Mini Portfolio Process
  • Choose one class or group of students and introduce a short-term portfolio process (e.g., for one unit or learning goal).
  • Include a rubric or reflection guide students will use.
  • Reflect on what you learned and what adjustments you’d make moving forward.
  • Feedback Inventory
  • Review and collect recent examples of feedback you’ve provided to students.
  • Analyze them for tone, specificity, and focus on progress.
  • Identify one change you’ll make to better support growth-oriented communication. How did this change impact your teaching?

Chapter 8 – Grading with Integrity: How Do We Get There?

Reflection Prompts

  • What practical steps can you take immediately to begin grading with integrity?
  • What potential resistance or challenges might you face when advocating for grading reform?
  • Reflect on the key strategies and recommendations from the authors that resonate most strongly with you. Activities:
  • Create an action plan for implementing grading with integrity in your classroom or school, including timelines, resources, and support needed.
  • Schedule and conduct a virtual meeting or forum with colleagues to discuss the implementation of grading with integrity practices and to collaboratively address anticipated challenges.
