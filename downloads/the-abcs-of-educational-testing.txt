1️⃣Why Fuss With Educational Testing?
Chapter 1: Why Fuss With Educational Testing?
Reflection Prompts:
● What is your current perception of educational testing?
● What experiences have shaped your beliefs about testing in schools?
● Why does Popham argue that increasing assessment literacy is essential for educators?
Application Activities:
● Assessment Audit: List all the assessments currently used in your classroom or school. Identify their
primary purpose.
● Professional Dialogue: Interview a colleague about their views on testing. Compare and contrast
perspectives.
● Educational Testing Inventory: Create a chart that shows which stakeholders (students, teachers, parents,
policymakers) benefit from different types of assessments.
2️⃣Three Primary Purposes of Ed Testing
Chapter 2: Three Primary Purposes of Educational Testing
Reflection Prompts:
● What are the three primary purposes of educational testing discussed by Popham?
● Which of these purposes is most prominent in your context?
● How might misalignment of purpose affect outcomes?
Application Activities:
● Purpose Alignment Worksheet: Select a recent assessment and determine if its design aligns with its
intended purpose.
● Staff Survey: Create and distribute a short survey asking staff what they believe is the purpose of key
assessments.
● Policy Review: Analyze a district or school testing policy to see how well it distinguishes between these
three purposes.
3️⃣Behind Standardized Testing’s Cloudy
Chapter 3: Behind Standardized Testing’s Cloudy Curtain
Reflection Prompts:
● What surprised you most about how standardized tests are developed?
● How does understanding test construction demystify the process?
● What are the limitations and strengths of standardized assessments?
Application Activities:
● Test Item Analysis: Analyze a standardized test item and identify its content, cognitive demand, and
potential biases.
● Research Task: Research the test development process of one standardized test used in your district and
create a one-page explainer.
● Presentation Slide: Design a visual slide explaining “How Standardized Tests Are Made” for a staff
meeting or PLC.
4️⃣Validity: The Crux of the Caper
Chapter 4: Validity: The Crux of the Caper
Reflection Prompts:
● What does validity mean in the context of testing?
● How can we determine if a test is valid for a specific purpose?
● What are some misconceptions about validity that you've seen in practice?
Application Activities:
● Validity Reflection Tool: Select a common assessment and evaluate its validity for its intended use.
● Case Study Creation: Write a short scenario where a test was used invalidly. Reflect on the
consequences.
● Faculty Workshop Design: Create a mini-PD session or handout explaining test validity and why it
matters.
5️⃣Reliability: Considering Consistency
Chapter 5: Reliability: Considering Consistency in Inconsistent
Containers
Reflection Prompts:
● How do you define reliability in assessment?
● Why can high reliability still result in an invalid test?
● In what ways does classroom variability challenge reliability?
Application Activities:
● Scoring Consistency Check: Review and compare how you and a colleague scored a student product to
assess inter-rater reliability.
● Rubric Revision: Update or create a rubric that helps improve consistency in scoring.
● Mini-Experiment: Give the same short quiz twice, spaced one week apart, and analyze score
consistency.
6️⃣Fairness in Testing: A Long Time Coming
Chapter 6: Fairness in Testing: A Long Time Coming
Reflection Prompts:
● What does fairness in testing mean to you?
● Which students might be disadvantaged by current testing practices?
● How can we proactively ensure fairness in test design and administration?
Application Activities:
● Bias Review Protocol: Use a fairness checklist to review a current assessment for bias or access issues.
● Student Voice Activity: Gather student feedback on how assessments feel to them (fair, stressful,

● Modify and Pilot: Revise a test item to increase fairness and test it with a small group of students.

Chapter 7: Reporting Results: Where Rubber and Road Meet

Chapter 8: Formative Assessment: Instructional Magic?
Reflection Prompts:

🏁 Final Reflection & Next StepsFinal Reflection and Action Plan
Synthesis:
Educational testing plays a powerful role in shaping instructional decisions, student opportunities, and
perceptions of learning. As Popham emphasizes, assessments are only as useful as educators’ understanding of
how to interpret and use them appropriately. Misinterpretation or misuse of test results—whether
classroom-based or large-scale—can lead to inaccurate conclusions about student learning and ineffective
instructional responses.
Assessment literacy, therefore, is not simply technical knowledge; it is a professional responsibility. When
educators understand concepts such as validity, reliability, and fairness, they are better equipped to evaluate the
quality of assessments, interpret results accurately, and make informed decisions that support student learning.
Effective use of assessment requires both critical analysis of test data and thoughtful application in instructional
contexts.
Ultimately, improving assessment literacy helps ensure that testing serves its intended purpose: to provide
meaningful information that supports learning, guides instruction, and promotes equitable outcomes for all
students.
