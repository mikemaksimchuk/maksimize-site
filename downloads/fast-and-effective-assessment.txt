1️⃣Why Grading for Integrity?
Chapter 1: More Effective Questioning
Big Idea
Effective questioning allows teachers to quickly gather evidence of student understanding while promoting deeper
thinking and engagement.
Reflection Questions
● Reflect on the types of questions you typically ask students during instruction. To what extent do these questions
reveal what students truly understand versus simply confirming surface-level knowledge?
● Think about a recent class discussion or lesson. Which questions prompted meaningful thinking or discussion
among students? What characteristics made those questions effective?
● How do you currently ensure that questioning strategies involve all students rather than just a few volunteers?
● In your classroom context, how might questioning strategies be improved to provide faster and more accurate
feedback about student understanding?
Application Activities
● Question Redesign: Review questions from a recent lesson and revise several to require explanation, reasoning,
or comparison.
● Participation Strategy: Implement a strategy that increases participation (random calling, mini whiteboards,
think-pair-share).
● Question Planning: Develop a sequence of questions for an upcoming lesson that moves from basic
understanding to deeper reasoning.
2️⃣A Brief History of Grading:
Chapter 2: Clear Learning Goals
Big Idea
When learning goals are clearly communicated, both teachers and students have a shared understanding of what success
looks like.
Reflection Questions
● Reflect on how learning goals are currently communicated in your classroom. How clearly do students understand
what they are expected to learn?
● Consider a recent lesson or unit. How did the learning goals influence the design of activities or assessments?
● How might unclear learning goals contribute to confusion or ineffective assessment?
● What strategies might help students take greater ownership of the learning goals in your classroom?
Application Activities
● Goal Clarity Exercise: Rewrite a current learning objective in student-friendly language.
● Success Criteria Development: Create success criteria that describe what achievement of the learning goal looks
like.
● Student Ownership: Ask students to explain the learning goal in their own words and reflect on their progress.
3️⃣Asking Why: What Is Our Purpose for Grading?
Chapter 3: Checking for Understanding
Big Idea
Frequent checks for understanding allow teachers to quickly identify misconceptions and adjust instruction before small
gaps become larger problems.
Reflection Questions
● Reflect on the strategies you currently use to check for understanding during instruction. How effectively do these
strategies reveal student thinking?
● Think about a time when you discovered a misunderstanding late in a unit or assessment. How might earlier
checks have helped address the issue sooner?
● What challenges prevent you from checking for understanding frequently during lessons?
● How might quick formative checks improve both student learning and instructional decision-making?
Application Activities
● Exit Ticket: Implement a brief exit ticket at the end of a lesson.
● Visual Response Strategy: Use quick-response strategies such as hand signals, mini whiteboards, or digital polls.
● Instructional Adjustment: Use evidence from a check-for-understanding activity to modify the next day’s
instruction.
4️⃣Current Grading Schemas
Chapter 4: Formal Formative Testing
Big Idea
Short, targeted formative assessments can provide meaningful evidence of learning without the heavy grading burden
associated with traditional tests.
Reflection Questions
● Reflect on the role formative assessments currently play in your classroom. How often do you use them to guide
instruction?
● Consider the design of your current quizzes or tests. To what extent do they provide actionable information about
student learning?
● How might shorter, more focused assessments provide clearer insights into student progress?
● What barriers exist in your context to implementing more frequent formative assessments?
Application Activities
● Short Quiz Design: Create a short formative quiz aligned to a key learning goal.
● Error Analysis: Use student responses from the quiz to identify common misconceptions.
● Instructional Planning: Adjust upcoming lessons based on the evidence gathered.
5️⃣Product: What is Standards-Based
Chapter 5: Sustainable Marking
Big Idea
Effective feedback does not require extensive marking on every assignment. Sustainable approaches to marking focus
feedback where it will have the greatest impact on learning.
Reflection Questions
● Reflect on the time you currently spend marking student work. Which aspects of marking provide the greatest
learning value for students?
● Consider feedback you have provided in the past. What types of feedback appear to influence student
improvement the most?
● How might alternative approaches to marking reduce workload while maintaining meaningful feedback?
● What strategies could help students take greater responsibility for reviewing and improving their own work?
Application Activities
● Focused Feedback: Provide feedback on only one or two key learning goals for an assignment.
● Peer Feedback: Implement a structured peer-review activity.
● Feedback Codes: Develop a simple feedback code system to streamline marking.
6️⃣Process: How Do We Include
Chapter 6: Reflection and Third-State Correction
Big Idea
Reflection and revision help students internalize feedback and improve their understanding of key concepts.
Reflection Questions
● Reflect on how students currently respond to feedback in your classroom. Do they revise their work or move on to
the next assignment?
● What structures exist in your classroom for students to reflect on their learning or correct mistakes?
● How might reflection activities help students develop stronger ownership of their learning?
● What strategies could help students view mistakes as opportunities for growth?
Application Activities
● Correction Opportunity: Allow students to revise an assignment after receiving feedback.
● Reflection Prompt: Ask students to write a brief reflection about what they learned from correcting mistakes.
● Learning Journal: Implement a regular reflection routine focused on learning progress.
🏁 Final Reflection & Action Plan
Final Reflection and Action Plan
Synthesis
Fast and effective assessment practices focus on gathering meaningful evidence of learning without overwhelming
teachers or students. Through effective questioning, clear learning goals, regular checks for understanding, focused
formative assessments, and sustainable feedback strategies, educators can create classrooms where assessment supports
learning rather than simply measuring it.
When students are encouraged to reflect on feedback and revise their work, assessment becomes an ongoing process that
promotes deeper understanding and continuous improvement.
