🌟 Introduction
Engaging Parents and Families in Grading Reform
By Thomas Guskey, PhD
Thank you for joining this professional learning experience!
This study plan follows the eight chapters of Engaging Parents and Families in
Grading Reforms by Thomas R. Guskey. Each chapter explores an important
dimension of grading reform, with particular attention to how schools can engage
parents and families as partners in understanding and improving grading and
reporting practices.
Participants are encouraged to engage with the book as part of this experience. You
may borrow a copy, access it in a way that works for you, or participate fully through
the reflection prompts and application activities provided here. Engagement with the
ideas—and thoughtful reflection on your own context—is what matters most.
This professional learning experience centers on the idea that meaningful grading
reform requires clarity of purpose, thoughtful communication, and collaboration with
families and other stakeholders. Throughout the chapters, participants will explore
how grading practices influence students, how families interpret grading information, and how schools can
build more transparent and meaningful reporting systems that accurately communicate student learning.
As you move through each chapter, record insights, identify questions and tensions, and reflect on how these
ideas connect to your current professional context. Consider how grading practices operate within your school
or district, and how they might evolve to better support student learning and family understanding. Dialogue
with colleagues is strongly encouraged as a way to extend thinking and collaboratively explore possible
changes.
Please don't hesitate to reach out if we can support you in any way to help you get the most out of this book
study experience. I'm excited to collaborate and make this journey both meaningful and enriching for you!

1️⃣Chapter 1
Chapter 1: Parents and Families’ Perspectives on
Grading and Reporting
Big Idea:
Understanding parents’ and families’ perspectives is essential when implementing grading reforms. Families
interpret grades through their own experiences with schooling, and their understanding shapes how grading
information is used to support students.
Reflection Questions
1. Reflect on the ways parents and families in your school community currently interpret grades and report
cards. What expectations or assumptions do they seem to hold about what grades represent? Provide
examples from conversations, conferences, emails, or feedback you have received from families.
2. Consider how grading practices influence the relationship between families and schools. In what ways
do current grading and reporting systems support or hinder families’ ability to understand and support
their child’s learning? Cite specific examples such as report card formats, grading scales, or
communication tools used in your context.
3. Think about a time when a family expressed confusion or concern about a grade or report card. What did
that interaction reveal about how grading information is interpreted outside of school? What evidence
from that situation might inform improvements to grading communication?
4. In what ways could families’ perspectives be more intentionally incorporated into conversations about
grading reform? Identify specific opportunities in your school or district where family input could
meaningfully shape grading practices or reporting systems.
Application Activities
● Conduct a brief survey or informal conversation with parents about how they interpret grades and report
cards.
● Facilitate a small parent focus group to explore their understanding of grading and reporting practices.
● Review report cards or grading communications currently used in your school and identify areas that
may create confusion for families.
2️⃣Chapter 2
Chapter 2: Understanding the Change Process
Big Idea:
Meaningful grading reform requires a thoughtful approach to change. Educators must understand how beliefs,
traditions, and institutional structures influence the adoption of new grading practices.
Reflection Questions
1. Consider the current attitudes toward grading practices within your school or district. How would you
describe the general level of openness or resistance to grading reform among educators, administrators,
students, and families? What experiences or evidence lead you to that conclusion?
2. Reflect on a past initiative or reform effort in your school. What factors helped the change succeed, and
what factors created barriers or resistance? How might those lessons apply to potential grading reforms?
3. What beliefs about grading appear most deeply rooted in your professional context? For example,
beliefs about fairness, motivation, accountability, or rigor. How have you observed these beliefs
influencing decisions about grading policies or classroom practices?
4. If grading reform were introduced in your school today, what specific barriers might arise? Consider
structural factors (policies, technology systems), cultural factors (traditions or expectations), and
relational factors (trust among stakeholders). What evidence supports your thinking?
Application Activities
● Draft a preliminary change-management plan outlining steps for introducing grading reform in your
context.
● Identify key stakeholders and consider how their perspectives might shape the implementation process.
● Map potential sources of resistance and propose strategies to address them.
3️⃣Chapter 3
Chapter 3: Clarifying the Purpose of Grading
Big Idea:
Grading practices should align with a clear and shared understanding of the purpose of grading. When the
purpose of grading is unclear, grading systems often combine multiple goals in ways that obscure student
learning.
Reflection Questions
1. Reflect on how grading is currently used in your school or classroom. What messages do grades
communicate to students and families about learning, effort, and achievement? Provide examples from
your grading policies, assignment structures, or report card systems.
2. In your experience, how do grading practices influence student motivation and learning behaviors?
Describe specific patterns you have observed among students in response to grades.
3. Consider whether your current grading practices primarily communicate student learning, compliance
with expectations, or a combination of both. What evidence from your classroom practices, grading
categories, or gradebook structure supports your thinking?
4. If grading were designed solely to communicate what students know and can do, how might current
practices in your school change? Identify specific examples where current policies or traditions may blur
the distinction between achievement and other factors.
Application Activities
● Draft a short mission statement for grading that reflects the purpose of grading in your educational
context.
● Analyze current grading policies or classroom practices to determine whether they align with that
purpose.
● Discuss the purpose of grading with colleagues and compare perspectives.
4️⃣Chapter 4
Chapter 4: Understanding Components of
Computerized Grading Systems
Big Idea:
Digital gradebooks and computerized grading systems influence how educators calculate and communicate
grades. Understanding these systems is essential for ensuring that technology supports accurate representations
of student learning.
Reflection Questions
1. Examine the structure of the electronic gradebook or grading system used in your school. How does the
system organize assignments, categories, and grade calculations? Provide examples from your own
gradebook configuration.
2. In what ways does the design of your grading software shape teachers’ grading practices? For example,
how do default settings, category weights, or averaging methods influence how grades are calculated?
3. Reflect on whether your grading system encourages practices that accurately represent student learning
or whether it reinforces traditional grading habits. What evidence from your own use of the system
illustrates this influence?
4. How might adjustments to grading software settings or practices improve the accuracy and clarity of
grades in your context?
Application Activities
● Analyze the grading calculations used in your electronic gradebook.
● Experiment with different gradebook configurations to see how they affect final grades.
● Collaborate with colleagues to evaluate whether your grading software supports your instructional goals.
5️⃣Chapter 5
Chapter 5: Designing Simple and Family-Friendly
Report Cards
Big Idea:
Report cards should clearly communicate student learning in ways that families can easily understand and use to
support their children.
Reflection Questions
1. Review the report card currently used in your school or district. What information does it communicate
effectively, and what information might be difficult for families to interpret? Provide specific examples
from the report card format.
2. Consider how families might interpret the information on your report card. What assumptions about
grading knowledge might the report card make that families may not share?
3. Reflect on feedback you have received from families about report cards or progress reports. What
themes or patterns have emerged from those conversations?
4. If you were redesigning your report card with families as the primary audience, what changes might
improve clarity and usefulness?
Application Activities
● Create a prototype of a simplified report card format.
● Gather feedback from families or colleagues about the clarity of the design.
● Develop a short guide explaining how to interpret the information on the report card.
6️⃣Chapter 6
Chapter 6: How Families Interpret Grading Labels
Big Idea:
The meaning of grading labels is not always interpreted consistently by families. Clear descriptors and shared
language are essential for effective communication.
Reflection Questions
1. Think about the grading labels used in your school (letter grades, percentages, proficiency scales, etc.).
How do educators interpret these labels compared to how families might interpret them?
2. Reflect on examples where families misunderstood or interpreted grades differently than intended. What
evidence from those situations reveals potential communication gaps?
3. How might cultural background, educational experiences, or language differences influence how
families interpret grading labels?
4. What additional information or descriptors might help families better understand what grades represent
in your context?
Application Activities
● Share examples of grading scales with families and ask how they interpret them.
● Develop written descriptors to accompany grading labels.
● Create informational materials explaining the grading scale used in your school.
7️⃣Chapter 7
Chapter 7: Replacing Old Traditions with Better
Practices
Big Idea:
Many grading practices persist because of tradition rather than evidence. Replacing outdated practices requires
reflection, collaboration, and a willingness to question long-standing assumptions.
Reflection Questions
1. Identify grading practices in your school that have been in place for many years. What reasons are
typically given for maintaining these practices?
2. Reflect on whether these traditions support accurate communication of student learning. What evidence
from your classroom or school suggests they may or may not serve this purpose?
3. Consider how students experience these grading traditions. How might these practices influence student
motivation, engagement, or perceptions of fairness?
4. What alternative practices could better align grading with the goal of accurately representing student
learning?
Application Activities
● Identify one grading tradition that could be reconsidered or revised.
● Research alternative approaches and discuss them with colleagues.
● Develop a short professional learning session to explore updated grading practices.
8️⃣Chapter 8
Chapter 8: Building a Coherent Reporting System
Big Idea:
A comprehensive reporting system should provide a clear and accurate picture of student learning and
development.
Reflection Questions
1. Reflect on how student progress is currently communicated in your school. What elements of learning
are emphasized, and what aspects may be missing?
2. How do different reporting tools (report cards, gradebooks, progress reports, conferences) work together
(or fail to work together) to communicate student learning?
3. What evidence suggests that families and students clearly understand the information provided in your
reporting system?
4. If you could redesign the reporting system in your school, what elements would you prioritize to ensure
clarity, accuracy, and usefulness?
Application Activities
● Design a model reporting system that communicates academic progress and other relevant aspects of
student development.
● Pilot elements of this system in your classroom or department.
● Collect feedback from families and colleagues to refine the design.
🏁 Final Reflection & Action Plan
Final Reflection and Action Plan
Synthesis:
Effective grading reform requires more than technical adjustments to policies or grading scales; it requires
thoughtful reflection on the purpose of grading and a commitment to clear communication with students and
families. When educators examine grading practices through the lens of transparency, accuracy, and fairness,
they create opportunities for grades to better reflect student learning and support student growth. Engaging
parents and families in conversations about grading helps build shared understanding and trust, ensuring that
grading reforms strengthen partnerships between schools and the communities they serve. Meaningful change in
grading practices is not simply about altering procedures; it is about developing a shared commitment to
practices that communicate learning clearly, support students, and promote collaboration among educators,
students, and families.
Personal Reflection and Action Plan:
● What are my top three takeaways from this book study? Reflect on the ideas, practices, or perspectives
from the book that most influenced your thinking about grading and reporting.
● How has this book study influenced my understanding of the purpose of grading and reporting?
Consider how your current beliefs and practices align with the principles discussed throughout the
chapters.
● What is one specific change I will make to my grading practices, reporting systems, or communication
with families? Identify a realistic step that could improve clarity, fairness, or transparency in how
student learning is communicated.
● What evidence from my current context suggests that this change is needed? Consider artifacts such as
grading policies, report cards, family communication, or feedback from students and parents.
● Who will support or hold me accountable as I implement this change? Identify colleagues, mentors, or
professional learning communities that can help you reflect on your progress and refine your approach.
● What is my timeline for implementing and reviewing this change? Outline when you plan to begin, how
long you will implement the change, and when you will revisit its effectiveness.
● How will I know if this change improves clarity and understanding of student learning for students and
families? Consider possible evidence such as improved communication, fewer misunderstandings about
grades, or feedback from families.

● What additional support or learning might I need to continue improving my grading and reporting
practices?
