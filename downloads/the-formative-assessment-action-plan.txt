Thank you for joining this professional learning experience.
This study plan follows the six chapters of The Formative Assessment Action
Plan by Nancy Frey and Douglas Fisher. Each chapter explores how teachers can
design and implement formative assessment practices that guide instruction,
improve student learning, and build a culture of continuous feedback.
Participants are encouraged to engage with the book as part of this experience.
You may borrow a copy, access it in a way that works for you, or participate fully
through the reflection prompts and application activities provided here.
Engagement with the ideas—and thoughtful reflection on your instructional
context—is what matters most.
This professional learning experience centers on the idea that formative
assessment is not a single event but a system of practices that helps teachers and
students continuously monitor progress toward learning goals. Through clear
goals, checks for understanding, meaningful feedback, and opportunities for reflection and next steps, educators
can create classrooms where assessment actively supports learning rather than simply measuring it.
As you move through each chapter, record insights, identify questions or tensions, and reflect on how these
ideas connect to your own classroom practices. Dialogue with colleagues is encouraged as a way to deepen
understanding and explore practical ways to strengthen formative assessment practices.

1️⃣Creating a Formative Assessment System
Chapter 1: Creating a Formative Assessment System
Big Idea
Formative assessment works best when it is implemented as a coherent system that continuously gathers
evidence of student learning and informs instructional decisions.
Reflection Questions
● Reflect on how formative assessment currently functions in your classroom. Is it primarily used to
monitor progress, adjust instruction, or evaluate learning outcomes?
● What evidence do you typically collect to determine whether students are learning during a lesson or
unit?
● Consider how formative assessment practices in your classroom are structured. Do they occur
consistently throughout instruction or mainly at the end of lessons?
● What barriers or challenges make it difficult to implement formative assessment as a continuous
instructional practice?
Application Activities
● Assessment Mapping: Identify where formative assessment currently occurs within a unit or lesson
sequence.
● Evidence Collection Plan: Design a plan for gathering evidence of student understanding during
different stages of instruction.
● Collaborative Discussion: Work with colleagues to identify strategies that support consistent formative
assessment practices.
2️⃣Feed-Up: Where Am I Going?
Chapter 2: Feed-Up: Where Am I Going?
Big Idea
Students learn more effectively when they clearly understand the learning goals and success criteria that guide
their work.
Reflection Questions
● Reflect on how learning goals are currently communicated in your classroom. How clearly do students
understand what they are expected to learn?
● Think about a recent lesson or unit. How did the learning goals shape instructional decisions and
assessment tasks?
● How might unclear learning goals affect student engagement or understanding?
● What strategies might help students take greater ownership of the learning goals?
Application Activities
● Goal Clarification: Rewrite a learning objective in student-friendly language.
● Success Criteria Development: Develop clear success criteria that describe what mastery of the
learning goal looks like.
● Student Reflection: Ask students to explain the learning goal and how they know when they have
achieved it.
3️⃣Checking for Understanding: Where Am I Now?
Chapter 3: Checking for Understanding: Where Am I Now?
Big Idea
Frequent checks for understanding allow teachers and students to identify learning progress and misconceptions
during instruction.
Reflection Questions
● Reflect on the strategies you currently use to check for understanding during lessons. How effectively do
they reveal student thinking?
● Think about a time when a misconception became apparent later in the learning process. How might
earlier checks for understanding have prevented that situation?
● What strategies could help ensure that all students participate in checks for understanding?
● How might quick formative assessment strategies help you adjust instruction more effectively?
Application Activities
● Exit Ticket: Implement a brief exit ticket to assess understanding at the end of a lesson.
● Participation Strategy: Use mini whiteboards, digital polls, or think-pair-share to quickly assess
understanding.
● Instructional Adjustment: Modify a lesson based on evidence gathered during a
check-for-understanding activity.
4️⃣Feedback: How Am I Doing?
Chapter 4: Feedback: How Am I Doing?
Big Idea
Effective feedback provides students with clear information about their progress and helps them understand how to
improve.
Reflection Questions
● Reflect on the types of feedback you typically provide to students. How specific and actionable is that feedback?
● Think about how students respond to feedback. Do they use it to improve their work, or do they move on to the
next assignment?
● How might feedback practices influence student motivation and confidence?
● What strategies might help students engage more actively with feedback?
Application Activities
● Focused Feedback: Provide feedback that addresses one or two specific learning goals.
● Peer Feedback: Implement structured peer review using clear criteria.
● Feedback Reflection: Ask students to write a brief response explaining how they will apply feedback.
5️⃣Feed-Forward: Where Am I Going Next?
Chapter 5: Feed-Forward: Where Am I Going Next?
Big Idea
Feed-forward strategies help students apply feedback and plan their next steps for improvement.
Reflection Questions
● Reflect on how students currently use feedback to guide their next steps in learning.
● Think about how often students revise work after receiving feedback. What structures support or limit
revision opportunities?
● How might feed-forward practices help students develop stronger learning habits?
● What strategies might help students identify their own next steps for improvement?
Application Activities
● Revision Opportunity: Allow students to revise work after feedback.
● Goal Setting: Ask students to set learning goals based on feedback received.
● Progress Monitoring: Have students track their learning progress across multiple assignments.
6️⃣Building a Formative Assessment System
Chapter 6: Building a Formative Assessment System
Big Idea
A strong formative assessment system integrates learning goals, checks for understanding, feedback, and next steps into
everyday teaching practices.
Reflection Questions
● Reflect on the formative assessment practices you have explored throughout this book study. Which strategies
appear most promising for your classroom?
● How might the different elements of formative assessment (feed-up, checking for understanding, feedback, and
feed-forward) work together in your instruction?
● What challenges might arise when implementing a more comprehensive formative assessment system?
● What supports or resources might help sustain formative assessment practices in your classroom or school?
Application Activities
● System Design: Develop a plan that integrates formative assessment strategies across a unit of instruction.
● PLC Collaboration: Share your formative assessment plan with colleagues and gather feedback.
● Instructional Reflection: Implement a formative assessment strategy and reflect on its impact on student
learning.
🏁 Final Reflection & Action Plan
Final Reflection and Action Plan
Synthesis
Formative assessment is most powerful when it is embedded within everyday teaching and learning practices.
By clearly communicating learning goals, regularly checking for understanding, providing meaningful
feedback, and helping students identify next steps, teachers create classrooms where assessment supports
continuous growth.
When students understand where they are going, where they are now, and how to move forward, assessment
becomes a process that strengthens both learning and motivation.
Personal Reflection and Action Plan
● What are my three most important takeaways from this book study?
● Which formative assessment practices already align with my teaching?
● Which strategies represent the greatest opportunity for improvement?
● What specific change will I implement in my formative assessment practices?
● What evidence will show that this change is improving student learning?
● Who will support or hold me accountable for implementing this change?
● What is my timeline for implementing and reviewing this change?
Next Steps

practices they will implement to strengthen formative assessment in their classrooms.
